About

Soft matter,
hard mathematics.

I am a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at UBC Vancouver, working at the intersection of computational mechanics, soft matter physics, and applied mathematics.

My research focuses on the hydrodynamics and microstructural dynamics of passive and active chiral nematics — liquid crystal phases in which molecular chirality couples with flow, geometric confinement, and activity to produce rich topological phenomena including defect nucleation, chaotic dynamics, and emergent collective flows.

For passive systems, I employ the Landau–de Gennes Q-tensor framework solved via finite element methods in COMSOL Multiphysics. For active chiral nematics, I use the Beris–Edwards formulation — coupling the Q-tensor evolution equation to the Navier–Stokes equations with an active stress tensor — solved using finite difference methods and Lattice Boltzmann methods (LBM). Simulation outputs are visualized and post-processed in ParaView.

Research focus

Two regimes,
one framework.

Passive systems

  • Cholesteric & nematic hydrodynamics
  • Confined channel flows
  • Sudden contraction geometries
  • Lid-driven cavity microstructure
  • Prosthetic hip joint lubrication
  • Director field evolution & defect patterns

Active systems

  • Active chiral nematics
  • Chirality–activity coupling
  • Spontaneous flow & chaotic dynamics
  • Topological defect nucleation & annihilation
  • Emergent collective motion
  • Confinement effects on active turbulence
